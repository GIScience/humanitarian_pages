Diverging Trends: Population Dynamics in Basins with Declining River Discharge

Interactive companion to the analysis, 1980–2019 · reconstructed river discharge (GLORIF1) × population (GHS-POP) on 4,734 HydroBASINS level-5 river basins

What am I looking at?

Rivers supply most of the freshwater people use. This dashboard shows, for every large river basin in the world, whether the amount of water flowing in its rivers declined between 1980 and 2019 — and how many people live in the affected basins. The headline finding: the population living in declining-discharge basins grew from 289 to 524 million (+81%). About 95% of that increase is simply global population growth; only a small remainder comes from population shifting toward (or away from) declining basins — and that remainder differs strongly by region.

How to read the map

  • Each colored shape is one river basin (HydroBASINS level 5; median size ≈ 17,500 km²). Click any basin for its details.
  • Use the view switcher (top-left of the map) to change what the colors mean. Each view has its own legend and explanation.
  • Country names can be toggled on/off and appear when you zoom in.

Three honest caveats (please read)

  1. “Discharge” is reconstructed, not directly measured everywhere. It comes from a hydrological model corrected with machine learning against river-gauge measurements (GLORIF1). Trends can locally reflect data artifacts.
  2. Everything is basin-aggregated. The underlying discharge grid is coarse (~50 km). Statements about single river reaches or small catchments are not possible.
  3. Exposure ≠ impact. Living in a declining-discharge basin does not automatically mean water shortage — infrastructure, groundwater and imports mediate actual impacts.

Map view

Exposed population over time ?

The primary definition weights each basin's population by its share of declining discharge cells (no arbitrary threshold). All three definitions roughly double over 40 years.

Why did exposure grow? ?

The global concentration effect, resolved ?

Positive bars push exposure up. The “between regions” bar means: regions that are already more exposed than average (South Asia, Sub-Saharan Africa, MENA) grew fastest. The small negative “within regions” bar means people did not, on net, move toward declining basins inside their region.

Mini-glossary

River discharge
The volume of water flowing through a river (m³/s). Here: monthly values on a global 0.5° grid, 1979–2019, reconstructed by the GLORIF1 dataset.
Declining / increasing
A statistically significant downward/upward trend of annual mean discharge over 1980–2019 (Mann–Kendall test with false-discovery-rate control — a standard guard against false positives when testing 62,464 cells at once).
Share of declining cells
The fraction of a basin's discharge grid cells that are “declining”. 0% = none, 100% = all. The primary exposure measure weights population by this share.
Compound hotspot
A basin that is in the top third worldwide for both discharge decline and population growth, and whose neighbors are too (tested with local join count statistics; only statistically significant cluster members are outlined).
LMDI decomposition
An accounting method (from energy economics) that splits a change into named contributions that add up exactly — no unexplained remainder.

This dashboard is a research companion, not an operational water-risk product. Basin values are statistical aggregates of a coarse (0.5°) reconstruction and must not be used for local planning decisions.
