## About Drinking Water Points

Maps publicly accessible drinking water — taps, fountains and filling points — across the area you select, and reports how densely they are distributed and how much of the area lies within a short walk of one. Public water is an important part of adapting to hotter summers and protecting public health during heat waves.

### How to read these results

Features come from OpenStreetMap via the ohsome API. The default categories are public drinking-water points (amenity=drinking_water) and potable taps/filling points; ornamental drinking fountains and springs can be switched on as extra layers. Explicitly non-potable (drinking_water=no) or private points are excluded, while untagged access is assumed public. Read the result as a count, a density per km2 and — where a population dataset is configured — per 1,000 inhabitants, plus a walking-distance coverage view. Coverage depends on OpenStreetMap completeness, which is uneven between places, so comparisons are fairest within one city.
