## About Heat resilience

Counts the places people can go for relief during hot weather — shaded parks, cool indoor spaces, public pools — together with the public drinking-water points that make staying outside bearable. It then asks who can actually reach them, and how provision compares with the number of residents in each neighbourhood.

### How to read these results

Heat relief comes in two kinds and a neighbourhood needs both: somewhere cool to be, and something to drink. This indicator maps each separately, then combines them so that an area scores well only if it has both — an area with parks but no water, or fountains but no shade, is scored by whichever it has least of.

Because walking range differs greatly between people, coverage is shown at three distances: 200 m (a realistic range for many older or less mobile residents during heat), 400 m (a comfortable walk) and 800 m (a determined one). The gap between the 200 m and 800 m maps is the population whose access depends on being able to walk.

Where population data is configured the indicator also shows provision per 1,000 residents, both by neighbourhood and as a continuous surface that does not depend on where administrative boundaries happen to fall. Results reflect OpenStreetMap completeness, which varies between places; drinking-water points in particular are unevenly mapped, so the data-quality panel is worth reading before comparing cities.
