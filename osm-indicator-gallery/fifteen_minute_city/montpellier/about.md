## About Everyday essentials on foot

Counts the everyday destinations people need most weeks — food shops, pharmacies, primary schools, health care, post and banking, and a park to sit in — and asks how much of the area has them within a short walk. The headline view is deliberately unforgiving: a neighbourhood counts as well served only if it has all of them nearby, not if it has many of one.

### How to read these results

The "15-minute city" is the idea that daily needs should be reachable on foot. This indicator tests that directly. It maps six kinds of everyday destination separately, then combines them so that each neighbourhood is scored by whichever kind it has least of — a place with several supermarkets but no pharmacy scores low, because for the person who needs a pharmacy it is not a short walk.

Coverage is shown at 800 m, roughly ten minutes at an unhurried pace, and again at 1200 m for a fifteen-minute walk. The difference between the two maps is the population whose access depends on being able and willing to walk a good distance.

Two limits are worth holding in mind. This measures how close things are, not whether people can actually use them: affordability, opening hours, accepting new patients, step-free entrances and whether a route feels safe are all invisible here. And it reflects OpenStreetMap completeness, which varies — health care and banking in particular are unevenly mapped, so read the data-quality panel before comparing one city with another.
