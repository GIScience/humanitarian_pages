## About Tactile Paving

Maps pedestrian crossings and kerbs fitted with tactile paving — the ridged or blistered surfaces that guide blind and partially sighted people. It is best read as a coverage indicator: what share of crossings and kerbs are barrier-free.

### How to read these results

Tactile paving is an attribute of crossings and kerbs in OpenStreetMap, not a place of its own, so this indicator maps the features that HAVE it (tactile_paving = yes, contrasted or primitive), split into crossings and kerbs. Crucially it is a completeness ratio, not a density: OpenStreetMap also records where tactile paving is absent (tactile_paving=no), so a raw count of equipped features partly reflects how thoroughly an area has been surveyed. Compare the equipped count against the total number of crossings and kerbs (in Heidelberg about 20% of crossings and 13% of kerbs are equipped) rather than reading it as features per km2.
