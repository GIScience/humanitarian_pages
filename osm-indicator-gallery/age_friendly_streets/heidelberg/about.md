## About Age-friendly streets

Counts the street features that decide whether a walk is possible for someone older or less mobile: benches to rest on, public toilets, dropped or tactile kerbs, and crossings that signal audibly. It reports them at the short distances that actually apply — 200 metres between rests, not the 800 metres a general walkability study would use.

### How to read these results

For many older residents a journey on foot is limited less by total distance than by what is available along the way. A bench every 200 metres turns an impossible walk into a possible one; a kerb that cannot be crossed ends it regardless of how close the destination is.

This indicator maps four such features separately and then scores each neighbourhood by whichever it has least of, because they are not substitutes: a street with plenty of benches and no crossable kerbs is not accessible. Coverage is shown at 200 metres and again at 400 metres, so the difference between the two maps shows how much a person's range changes what is available to them.

Two cautions. Tactile paving and audible signals are mapped very unevenly in OpenStreetMap — an empty map here often means nobody has surveyed it, not that the features are absent, so read the data-quality panel first. And the things this cannot see — gradient, pavement width, surface condition, lighting — matter as much as the things it can. Treat a good score as "the countable features are present", not as "this is comfortable to walk".
